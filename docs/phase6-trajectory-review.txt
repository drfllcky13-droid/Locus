2026-09-23

The bloodstain and camera/height tools will copy what is described here. Please review it
before they are built.


1. WHAT'S BUILT

Ground-truth generators (all three tools), `locus-validate gen-* --out DIR`:
  - gen-trajectory: a straight path through three panels (vehicle door 1 mm at 35°, interior
    wall 12.5 mm at 22°, wardrobe side 18 mm at 12°). It gives the true entry and exit
    centres, picks with 2 mm in-plane and 1 mm scan noise, a probe rod with random play
    within what its hole allows, and the panels as an E57 with the holes cut out.
  - gen-bloodstain: droplets from a known origin onto the floor and walls, flying either
    straight or ballistic (gravity plus drag). It gives each stain's true impact and
    directional angles; the measured ellipse, with edge noise of 0.1 mm + 1.5 % per edge;
    stain photos at 20 px/mm with three fiducials each; and the surfaces as an E57.
    Measured impact angles come out about 7° RMS at 20–60° for these 2–5 mm stains, and
    about 12° at 70–80°, where asin(w/l) is ill-conditioned.
  - gen-camera: a room with 30 control markers and three standing people (1.63, 1.84 and
    1.72 m), rendered through two cameras: a CCTV camera with strong barrel distortion and
    a handheld photo. It gives each marker's true and picked pixels and each person's head
    and feet pixels, and the room as an E57. The people are built from Drillis & Contini
    proportions (via Winter 2009) with stature defined as floor to top of head, no
    footwear. The person model in the app still uses its older ratios; it will be brought
    in line, with the citation, before height analysis, as you asked.

The analysis framework (shared by all tools):
  - Schema 6, table `analyses`. A run is one immutable record: tool, method version
    ("trajectory/1"), name, the run as JSON, the SHA-256 of that JSON, the run it revises
    (if any), and who ran it and when. The database refuses edits and deletes.
  - The run JSON holds:
      inputs        every pick, re-resolved in Rust from stored data, with its uncertainty
                    and any plane fitted around it
      parameters    everything the examiner set
      results       with 1σ uncertainties, plus the fit's diagnostics
      assumptions   stored as text with the run
      limitations   likewise
      summary       one line, for lists and the audit log
  - Withdrawing a run needs a reason, which is audit-logged. The record stays, marked.
  - Audit actions: analysis.created, analysis.withdrawn, analysis.reported (with the PDF's
    path and hash).
  - Preview vs save: the UI previews live from the backend (same code as the saved run),
    and nothing is stored until "Save analysis".

The report pattern:
  - One Typst layout for every tool (templates/analysis.typ). Title block: project, record
    name and id, method version, record SHA-256, who ran it and when, who printed it and
    when, app version, and the audit log's head hash.
  - Then a red "Needs attention" box when anything warrants it: withdrawn, χ² inflation,
    the fit's cone wider than the drawn cone, or everything resting on one thin surface.
  - Then the tool's sections, built only from the stored record, so a report can be
    reprinted any time and says exactly what was stored. Block types: text, key–value
    pairs, tables, lists, and simple vector figures (plan and side views with a scale
    bar).
  - The template computes nothing; every number is formatted in Rust, and tests check the
    PDF's text against the run.
  - Trajectory sections: Result, Points used (with residuals), Angles to each surface,
    Possible muzzle positions (with plan and side figures), Method, Assumptions,
    Limitations.

The trajectory tool:
  - Maths: a weighted total-least-squares line in travel order. The direction's
    covariance is propagated to first order from every picked coordinate. It is inflated
    by the Birge ratio when the residuals exceed the stated σ (a χ² test), and rod play is
    added in quadrature. The 95 % cone comes from χ²₂. Bearing and elevation are given
    with 1σ, plus angles to each surface (impact, horizontal, vertical, with 1σ).
  - Possible muzzle positions: the centre line and a user-set cone (default ±5°) traced
    back through a height band above a picked floor, and the band's footprint in plan.
  - UI (3D view side panel): choose defects or a probe rod, click "Pick the next point"
    (entry and exit alternate, surfaces are named, σ is set per pick), set the band, cone
    and floor (the floor can be picked). The preview shows the result, the warnings, each
    point's residual and the per-surface angles. The path, cone edges and footprint are
    drawn in 3D. "Save analysis" stores it; the saved list has Report PDF… and
    Withdraw….
  - Method note: docs/methods/trajectory.md (formulas, validation, assumptions,
    limitations, references).


2. RESULTS

  Acceptance (0.5°):  the worst of 50 generated scenes was 0.066°.
  Calibration:        the true direction was inside the stated 95 % cone in 94.5 % of
                      400 runs.
  Monte Carlo:        the stated σ of bearing and elevation agrees with the spread of
                      4,000 repeats within 10 %.
  In the app:         picking the generated scene's defects on its point cloud gave
                      0.105°. I clicked 8 mm beside each hole's centre, so the χ² test
                      (47 on 8 degrees of freedom) widened the uncertainty 2.4×, as it
                      should.
  Probe rod:          recovered within its play every time, and the cone always covers
                      the play.

3. A BUG THIS FOUND (fixed; affects measurements too)

The in-app check picked a wall 1.5 m behind the panel I clicked. Point sprites are capped
at 8 px, so close up there are gaps between a surface's points. The old pick took
whichever drawn pixel was nearest the cursor, even when that pixel was a farther surface
seen through a gap. A pick now prefers the nearest surface in the pick window (within
1 cm + 2 % of the nearest candidate's depth), then the point nearest the cursor. This
changes Phase 2 picking behaviour for every tool. It is logged in DECISIONS.md and tested.


4. DECISIONS FOR YOU (before bloodstain and height copy the pattern)

  a. Hole centres. A hole's centre has no scan points, so a pick lands on the rim or
     beside it. Today the examiner states a larger σ for that. The alternative is a
     "defect centre" helper: click near the hole, the tool fits an ellipse to the rim
     points in the surface's plane, and it reports the centre with its own uncertainty
     (it would also give the defect's long axis as a second estimate of direction). Worth
     adding now, or later?

  b. Conventions for the angles to a surface. Horizontal is + to the right and vertical is
     + upward, both seen from the shooter's side and measured from the surface's normal.
     Bearing is clockwise from project +y; elevation is + up. Do these match your lab's
     reporting, or should the report use another convention (for example, angles from the
     surface plane rather than from its normal, or compass bearings once a project has
     true north)?

  c. Two cones. The report shows the fit's 95 % cone and uses a separate examiner-set
     cone (default ±5°, as the spec says) for muzzle positions, with a warning when the
     fit's cone is wider. Keep both, or use max(fit, stated)?

  d. Defaults. Height band 0.9–1.8 m above the floor, trace-back range 30 m, pick σ 2 mm
     (defects) or 1 mm (rod ends), rod play 2°, surface plane radius 5 cm.

  e. Record wording. Assumptions and limitations are stored as text inside each run, so
     an old record keeps the wording it was made with. Changing that wording, like
     changing a formula, means a new method version ("trajectory/2").

  f. Report contents. Is anything missing that your reviewers expect? For example: a
     free-text examiner's note per run, a screenshot of the 3D view, photographs of the
     defects, or the picks' scan ids and record numbers (they are in the stored record
     but not printed).
