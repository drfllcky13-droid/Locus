1. TRAJECTORY DECISIONS (applied)

  - Hole-centre helper: an ellipse is fitted to the scan points round each hole's rim.
    - The centre is reported with its uncertainty. The ellipse's impact angle is reported
      as an independent cross-check, and a disagreement is flagged.
    - A manual centre (the clicked point) needs a reason, which is stored and printed.
    - In the app, picking the generated scene's six defects now gives 0.008° from the
      truth; it was 0.105° with the clicked points themselves.
  - The cross-check tests agreement on the axis ratio b/a against the sine of the path's
    impact angle, not on the angles themselves. The in-app check found why: with only
    about 15 rim points on a 9 mm hole, the angle's error is lopsided near 90°. An angle
    test flagged a hole whose ratio was 1.6σ from expected. The report explains this, and
    that the ellipse angle is weak evidence for small holes.
  - Angles are reported two ways, each convention a per-run setting printed in the method
    section:
    - surface-relative: vertical up or down from level, and horizontal left or right of
      perpendicular, viewed facing the surface;
    - scene-relative: a bearing from project north or a named axis.
  - Both cones are kept:
    - "Measurement uncertainty (95 %, computed)", in blue;
    - "Examiner-defined zone (±X°, analyst judgment)", in orange.
    They use the same colours in the plan and elevation figures and in the 3D view.
  - Report additions:
    - plan and elevation figures;
    - a points table with centre method, coordinates, and the source scan point (scan,
      record, revision);
    - hole fits with the cross-check, and manual centres with their reasons;
    - defect photos, hash-checked;
    - the case number (a project setting, on every analysis report);
    - sign-off lines for the examiner and the technical review.

2. BLOODSTAIN AREA OF ORIGIN (built)

  Workflow, per stain, in an editor beside the 3D view:
  - Choose the stain's photo from the image evidence.
  - Point pairs: click a pixel, then the same point on the scan. Two pairs place the photo
    on the surface; three or more check it with residuals.
  - Opacity slider: shows the photo on the scan.
  - Edge: automatic (seed click plus an adjustable threshold) or clicked points.
  - Tail: click the tail tip.
  - Exclude, with a reason.

  The backend:
  - resolves every pick from stored data;
  - fits each ellipse, with the tail trimmed;
  - computes impact asin(w/l) and direction, with 1σ;
  - fits the origin, with a residual for each stain, χ², and a bootstrap 95 % ellipsoid.

  The report contains:
  - the straight-line caveat;
  - the origin, its height and the 95 % region;
  - plan and elevation figures;
  - every stain's angles, whether it was used, and its residual;
  - the photos, alignments and edges, with hashes and scan points;
  - method, assumptions, limitations and sign-off.

  Method note: docs/methods/bloodstain.md.

  Validation (Phase 6 criterion: mean error under 10 cm on clean synthetic data):

    Stain photos (generator), alignment + automatic edge + tail, 8 rooms
      mean 0.6 mm, worst 1.0 mm; truth inside the 95 % region 8 of 8
    In the app, six photos aligned by clicking fiducials on the cloud
      11.5 mm; region 8 x 5 x 3 cm; chi2 0.4 on 9
    Hand-measurement noise (0.1 mm + 1.5 % per edge; small, near-round stains), 40 rooms
      mean 233 mm; truth inside the region 95 % of the time
    Under gravity, with every stain included
      the straight-line origin is 1.7-2.4 m too high

3. WHERE I DEPARTED FROM THE SPEC (please confirm or redirect)

  a. The origin is fitted on the stains' measured angles, not on the rays' perpendicular
     distances.
     - What the spec says: the least-squares point on perpendicular distances.
     - What it is now: the point whose paths best match each stain's impact angle and
       direction, weighted by their 1σ. The perpendicular-distance point is only the
       starting value.
     - Why: a direction error makes a ray miss the true origin on one side only.
     - Effect in validation: the distance-based point was 12-32 cm off, with its region
       covering the truth 0-82 % of the time.

  b. "Upward-moving" is applied with its uncertainty.
     - A wall stain is used only if its direction is upward by more than 2σ. Floor,
       downward and uncertain stains are left out unless included with a reason.
     - With hand-measurement noise, 14 % of the stains that read as upward were really
       moving down. They pulled the origin about 20 cm low, and no resampling can see
       that.
     - The analyst's mark is the tail they click.

  c. The 95 % region uses a Hotelling T² radius, not chi-square, because the covariance
     comes from the same stains. With 30 stains, chi-square covered the truth 88 % of the
     time. At least 4 stains are required.

  d. The automatic edge's systematic error (0.2 % per axis, 0.1° on orientation) was
     measured on the generator's photos, which are clean, synthetic and 20 px/mm.
     -> It should be re-measured on real casework photos before release.

  e. Photos are placed by a similarity (no perspective), so they must be taken square on,
     with a scale in the surface's plane.
     -> If your lab photographs stains at an angle, I can add a four-point perspective
        (homography) option.

  f. Floor stains are never "upward", so they are excluded by default.
     -> If you use floor stains for a 2-D area of convergence, say so. That would be a
        separate, plan-view result.
