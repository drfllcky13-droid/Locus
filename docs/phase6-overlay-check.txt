Checking the bloodstain photo overlay in the app
2026-09-23 (build 2faaa4e or later)

WHAT WAS WRONG

  The blank close-ups were a real rendering bug, not the overlay.
  - Cause: the eye-dome lighting pass treated any pixel nearer than 1 m to the camera as
    "nothing drawn" and painted it as background. It used a negative log depth as that
    test, and log depth is negative under 1 m.
  - Effect: at 1 m or closer, the scan vanished, and so did the photo overlay.
  - Fix: the pass now tests the stored depth itself.
  - Near-plane clipping: not the cause. The near plane is 5 mm.
  - Depth fighting between the photo and the points: guarded against anyway. The photo is
    lifted off the wall by three times the scan's RMS roughness there (at least 1 mm), and it
    now writes depth.

  Scripted screenshots at 1 m, 0.35 m and 0.15 m now show the scan and the photo; before, all
  three were blank.

STEPS (about 10 minutes)

  Test data: the generated bloodstain room, already on disk.
    Scan:   E:\Claude\scratch\locus\gt\bloodstain\scene.e57
    Photos: E:\Claude\scratch\locus\gt\bloodstain\photos\stain-246.png (square on)
            E:\Claude\scratch\locus\gt\bloodstain\photos\stain-246w.png (the same photo
            warped as if taken 35° off square on)
    Each photo has three small black crosses (fiducials) at three corners of a square. They
    are the points to pair.

  1. Open the app, create a new project, and import scene.e57 plus both photos.

  2. Look closely at any wall with no tool open.
     Orbit in and scroll until the wall is well under 1 m away.
     - Expect: the scan stays visible all the way in. Very close, it breaks into separate dots
       (points are drawn at most 8 px wide), but never goes blank.
     This is the fix itself; it applies everywhere in the viewer, not only to bloodstain.

  3. Open "Bloodstain area of origin", then "New area of origin…".
     Choose stain-246 and click "Add stain", then "Edit on photo…".

  4. Find the stain on the scan.
     It is on the wall y = 3.5 m, about x = 0.14 m, 2.1 m up.
     Get close enough that the three fiducials' positions are clear.

  5. Pair the photo with the scan.
     In "Point pairs" mode, three times: click a fiducial in the photo, then the same spot on
     the scan.
     - Expect: after the second pair, the photo appears on the wall.
     - Expect: after the third, the status line reads about "20 px/mm; pairs fit to 0.x mm
       RMS".

  6. The check itself.
     a. Set "Photo on the scan" to about 0.5.
        Orbit to about 30 cm from the wall, then to 10 cm.
        - The photo should sit flat on the wall, not blink or break up as you move.
        - The scan's dots should show through only as much as the opacity lets them.
     b. Set the opacity to 1.
        - The photo should cover the scan completely.
     c. Orbit behind the wall.
        - The photo should be hidden by the wall, not float in front of it.

  7. The perspective correction.
     Add a second stain from stain-246w, pair its three fiducials the same way, then open
     "Scale corners".
     a. Type 36.3 and 36.3 mm as the width and height.
     b. Click the four corners of the fiducial square in order around it. The fourth corner is
        where the square would close; it has no cross. Estimate it.
        - Expect: a purple outline and the status "perspective corrected (stretch 22 %…)".
        - Expect: a red line about the large correction (the photo is 35° off square on).
          That warning is correct.
     c. Look at the overlay.
        - The warped photo should now lie on the wall as a square, the same as stain-246.
        - Its fiducials should sit on the scan fiducials you paired.

  8. Optional: finish the stains to see the whole run.
     For each stain, choose "Edge: automatic" and click inside the stain, then "Tail" and
     click beyond the pointed end.
     - Expect: both photos give an impact angle near 39.6° (the true value).

WHAT TO REPORT BACK

  - Any blank or blinking view at step 2 or step 6.
  - A photo that sits off its fiducials, or shows through a wall from behind.
