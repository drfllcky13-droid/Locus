1. BLOODSTAIN DECISIONS (applied)

  - The fit in angles stays primary. Beside it: the conventional ray-distance point, with its
    own 95 % region and its distance from the result.
    - The report's method section explains why, cites Camana 2013 and Illes & Boué 2013, and
      gives the validation comparison on the same stains:
        photos       0.6 mm, region covers 8/8      against   4.8 mm, 0/8
        hand noise   233 mm, 95 %                   against   302 mm, 92 %
    - Each stain's share of the fit is shown. "Needs attention" names the near-round stains
      (70° or more) when they carry over half of it.
  - Floor stains: an optional plan-view convergence.
    - It is a separate 2-D result with its own section and figure; the 3-D origin is
      unchanged by it.
    - In a test with 3° direction noise, its 95 % ellipse covered the truth in 92.5 % of 200
      runs.
    - Limit: the 3-D origin must still be computable, so a run of floor stains alone is not
      supported yet. Say if you need it.
  - Four-point perspective correction from the scale's corners.
    - The corner-click error is carried to each stain by Monte Carlo.
    - Flagged: a stretch over 10 % (about 25° off square on), and a scale size that disagrees
      with the scan by more than the pairs' own uncertainty allows.
    - In the app, with photos warped 35° off square on and corrected: impact angles within 1°
      of the truth, origin 27 mm from the truth.
    - The in-app run is also why the second flag checks against the pairs' uncertainty. Scan
      points snap within their ~3 mm spacing, so on a 35 mm scale the pairs alone were 1–10 %
      off. A fixed 2 % test would have fired on nearly every photo.

4. CAMERA MATCHING, HEIGHT, WITNESS PERSPECTIVE (built)

  Person model:
  - Drillis and Contini stature fractions, cited; the same as the camera generator.
  - Exactly its stature, soles to the top of the head.

  Camera solve:
  - Six or more pixel–scan pairs.
  - Start: the DLT on the pairs nearest the image centre.
  - Refinement: Levenberg–Marquardt in stages. You choose the lens model: focal length only;
    + k1; + k1, k2; or full, with the principal point and k1–k3, p1, p2.
  - Pose and lens are reported with 1σ, plus each pair's residual, RMS and χ², with warnings.

  Heights:
  - Mark the feet and the head; the height comes by reverse projection onto the floor.
  - A Monte Carlo over the camera's covariance and the picks gives 1σ and a 95 % interval.
  - A person model of that height is drawn over the photo and in 3D. You can set its height,
    pose and facing to match the frame by eye, then use its head point; the report says so.

  Photo over the scene:
  - The 3D view can look through the solved camera, with the photo drawn over it through
    the lens distortion and an opacity slider.
  - In the photo editor, the scan and each pair's residual are drawn over the photo.

  Witness:
  - Pick where they stood, state the eye height, pick where they looked, and set the field of
    view.
  - Lines of sight to picked targets come out clear or blocked, with where. You can view from
    the eye.

  Each tool has a PDF report: result, pairs table with scan sources, heights, the photo
  (hash-checked), method, assumptions, limitations, references and sign-off.

  Validation (generator: a wide-angle CCTV with strong distortion, and a handheld photo; three
  people 1.63, 1.84 and 1.72 m):
    40 rooms × 2 cameras × 3 people    mean height error 6.0 mm; with a good solve, 95 % of
                                       errors under 14 mm, worst 28 mm; 95 % interval covers 92 %
    In the app (CCTV, 14 pairs)        heights +9.8, +11.6, +7.3 mm (1σ 13–17 mm)

  The validation found and fixed two real problems:
    a. The generator placed a marker 67° off-axis in the middle of the CCTV image. The
       distortion polynomial folds back past a certain radius. The generator and the solver
       now treat points past that radius as not seen.
    b. A one-step solve from the DLT fell into a wrong minimum on the wide-angle lens. It is now
       staged.

5. YOUR CALLS

  a. The spec's height bound, "under 2 cm with a good camera solve", I read as: 95 % of errors
     under 2 cm when the solve states the height to 1 cm (1σ). That gives 14 mm. The worst of
     211 such cases was 28 mm, about 2.8σ, which is statistically expected at that count.
     -> Confirm, or give the bound you want.
  b. Lens model default: "focal length, k1, k2", which holds the principal point at the image
     centre. The full model is more accurate but needs about 14 or more pairs.
     -> Should the default be full when there are at least 14 pairs?
  c. Pairs all on one plane (for example a CCTV that sees only the floor) are refused. A
     planar start (from a homography) can be added if your cameras often see only one surface.
  d. Eye height is only what the examiner states. I found no verified source for an eye-height
     fraction of stature to offer as a default, so none is given.
  e. Line of sight defaults: blocked by 3 or more scan points within 30 mm of the line,
     ignoring 100 mm at each end. -> Adjust if your practice differs.
  f. Not done yet:
     - exporting the witness view or the look-through view as an image for the report;
     - video (choosing a frame, rolling shutter);
     - drawing the whole scan over the photo (only the points loaded at the time are drawn).
     -> Say which you need now.
